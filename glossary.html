Glossary

The industry’s words, defined without a second glossary.

Updated 9 November 2026

ABCDEFGHKLMNOPRSTUVW

A

Address
A string that identifies where coins can be sent. It is derived from a public key and can be shared freely.
Airdrop
A distribution of tokens to existing addresses, usually to reward early users or to advertise a new project.
AMM
Automated market maker. A contract that prices trades with a formula and a pool of two assets, instead of an order book.

B

Bear market
A long stretch of falling prices and low appetite for risk. The opposite of a bull market.
Block
A bundle of transactions with a header that links it to the block before it.
Block explorer
A public website that lets you search blocks, addresses and transactions on a chain.
Bridge
A service that moves value from one chain to another, usually by locking it on one side and issuing a claim on the other.

C

Centralised exchange
A company that holds customer balances and matches trades on its own books. Convenient, and a counterparty.
Cold wallet
A wallet whose key is kept offline, so a compromised browser cannot reach it.
Confirmation
One block mined on top of the block that contains a transaction. More confirmations mean more work would be needed to reverse it.
Consensus
The process by which independent computers agree on one history of transactions.
Custody
Who controls the key. Self-custody means you do. Custodial means a company does, and you hold a claim.

D

DeFi
Decentralised finance. Lending, trading and borrowing through contracts, without a company in the middle.
DEX
A decentralised exchange. Trades settle on-chain from your own wallet, usually against a pool or an order book protocol.
Difficulty
The threshold a block hash must beat. It adjusts so blocks keep arriving at the target pace.

E

Ethereum
A general purpose chain that runs contracts. It uses accounts rather than bitcoin’s outputs, and it charges gas for computation.
EVM
The Ethereum Virtual Machine, the environment that executes contracts. Many other chains copy it so the same tools work.

F

FDV
Fully diluted valuation. Price multiplied by the maximum supply, including tokens not yet in circulation.
Fiat
Government issued money, such as dollars or euros. Not backed by a commodity.
Fork
A change to the rules. A soft fork keeps old nodes compatible; a hard fork splits the network into two histories.

G

Gas
The fee paid for computation on a contract chain. It is priced in the chain’s own token and rises when the network is busy.

H

Halving
The cut in the block reward that bitcoin makes every two hundred and ten thousand blocks, roughly every four years.
Hardware wallet
A device that keeps the private key offline and signs transactions after showing the details on its own screen.
Hash
A fixed length fingerprint of any data. Change the data and the hash changes; the reverse is not possible.
Hot wallet
A wallet on an internet connected device. Fast, and exposed to whatever reaches that device.

K

KYC
Know your customer. Identity checks that regulated exchanges run before they let you deposit or withdraw.

L

Layer 2
A network built on top of a main chain that settles its results there, trading some guarantees for speed and lower fees.
Ledger
The record of transactions. In crypto it is replicated across many machines rather than kept by one bookkeeper.
Liquidation
The forced closing of a leveraged position when its margin falls below the exchange’s requirement.
Liquidity
The ability to trade size without moving the price. Deep books have it; thin books do not.

M

Market cap
Price multiplied by circulating supply. What the last trade implies, not what every holder could withdraw.
Mempool
The pool of valid transactions waiting to be included in a block.
Mining
Proposing blocks by spending computation. On bitcoin, the winning miner collects the block reward and fees.
Multisig
A setup where several keys must approve a transaction before it can be sent. Removes a single point of failure.

N

Node
A computer that keeps a copy of the chain and checks new blocks against the rules.
Nonce
A number in a block header that miners change to steer the hash.

O

Oracle
A service that feeds outside data, such as a price or a weather reading, into a contract.

P

Perpetual
A futures contract with no expiry date, kept near spot by periodic funding payments between longs and shorts.
Private key
The secret number that signs a transaction. Whoever has it controls the coins.
Proof of stake
A consensus method where validators lock capital and lose it if they sign conflicting histories.
Proof of work
A consensus method where miners spend computation to propose blocks, making history expensive to rewrite.

R

Reorg
A replacement of recent blocks by a longer valid chain. Routine at one block of depth, serious at many.
Rug pull
A project that takes deposits and removes the liquidity or mints new supply, leaving buyers unable to sell.

S

Seed phrase
Twelve or twenty four words that encode the master key of a wallet. Anyone with them controls every address derived from it.
Self-custody
Holding the key yourself. No counterparty can freeze the balance, and no support line can recover a lost key.
Smart contract
Code deployed on a chain that runs when it is called, and holds funds according to its rules.
Stablecoin
A token designed to track a reference asset, usually a currency. The quality depends on what backs it.
Staking
Locking tokens to help secure a proof of stake chain, in exchange for rewards and the risk of slashing.

T

Testnet
A separate chain used for development, where coins have no market value.
Token
An asset issued on an existing chain, rather than the chain’s own native coin.

U

UTXO
Unspent transaction output. Bitcoin’s way of holding value: a set of coins that a key can spend, one transaction at a time.

V

Validator
A participant that proposes or attests to blocks on a proof of stake chain.
Volatility
How far and how fast a price moves. High volatility makes leverage expensive and stop orders unreliable.

W

Wallet
Software or hardware that stores keys and signs transactions. It does not store the coins themselves.
