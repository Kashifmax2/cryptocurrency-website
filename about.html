About the desk

Chainwise explains how crypto works to people who would rather understand it than be sold it.

Published 9 November 2026

What this is

Chainwise is an independent publication about crypto mechanics. It covers how chains settle, how custody works, how markets price risk and how the common scams operate. It does not publish price targets, coin promotions or sponsored guides.

The site is written as a set of long pieces rather than a feed. Each guide is meant to be read once and then used as a reference. When a mechanic changes, the guide is revised and the update date moves.

How guides are written

Explanations are built from primary material: protocol documentation, source code, exchange rulebooks, court filings and public chain data. Where a claim depends on a number, the number is dated and attributed rather than left floating.

We avoid predictions. A guide can say what a mechanism does under stress, and it can say what would have to be true for a strategy to work. It cannot know what a price will do, and it does not pretend to.

Corrections

When a guide is wrong, the correction is published on the page rather than quietly edited. Send the passage, the source and the date. A correction that changes the meaning of a paragraph is noted at the foot of that guide with the date it changed.

Advertising

Display advertising pays for the desk. Ad units are sold by third party networks, are labelled Advertisement, and are kept out of the body of a guide. An advertiser has no influence over what a guide says, and a guide is never written around an ad.

Chainwise does not accept paid coverage, paid rankings or paid links in editorial text. If that changes, the change will be stated here before it appears on a page.

Independence

The desk holds no tokens it writes about, and takes no compensation from projects covered in a guide. Any future exception would be disclosed in the piece itself, at the top, in plain language.

No advice

Nothing on Chainwise is investment, legal or tax advice. Crypto assets are volatile and can lose all of their value. Self-custody puts the responsibility for keys, backups and approvals on you.

Contact

Corrections, tips and advertising enquiries go through the contact page. Editorial correspondence is read by the desk, not by an agency.
